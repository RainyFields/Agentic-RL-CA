Rubric error patterns — GRPO step-75 rollouts

2026-08-14 · 2,000 trajectories (34,125 judged search turns) · policy token_grpo_qwen3-8b-base_32turn_fast75_s0 @ step 75 · judge gpt-oss-120b (temp 0, JSON-schema mode, 0 fallbacks) · greedy eval decoding on NQ / TriviaQA

1 Key findings

  • The dominant error pattern is a repeated-query attractor, not bad querying. First searches are nearly flawless (0.8% non-novel, 100% progression-valid); from search #3 onward, 99.96% of queries are non-novel and 99.98% retrieve nothing new. Virtually every trajectory (99.95%) contains a run of ≥3 consecutive non-novel searches.
  • Evidence sufficiency arrives almost immediately; the policy keeps searching anyway. 70.5% of trajectories reach judge readiness p≥0.9, on average after 1.02 searches — then issue 16.1 more. Two-thirds (66.3%) of all search turns happen after sufficiency.
  • The judge's readiness and info-gain signals discriminate success; well-formedness does not. Final-turn p predicts EM with AUC 0.74 and mean info_gain with AUC 0.69, while well_formed fails on 92.6% of turns uniformly across successes and failures (AUC 0.49) — the policy pastes the question verbatim as its query, on winning and losing trajectories alike.
  • Under greedy decoding the policy searches 17.1× per rollout (median 17) — vs ~5.4 at temp 1.0 in training — so these numbers are an upper bound on the pathology; the temp-1.0 training-matched collection (ckpts 25/50/75) is in flight to confirm.

2 Setup

Per-turn rubric judging of the existing step-75 GRPO eval rollout dump (p8b_eval_grpo_s75, greedy decoding, 32-turn horizon). One gpt-oss-120b call per trajectory grades every search turn on five binary criteria (well_formed, relevant, novel, progression, info_gain) and estimates readiness pt — the probability the gold answer is derivable from evidence retrieved in turns 1..t — plus sufficiency_turn (first t with p ≥ 0.9). The judge sees gold answers (training-time privilege) and never sees the agent's <think> text.

3 Over-search: sufficiency vs behavior

The judge says evidence sufficient to answer arrives after 1.02 searches on average (for the 70.5% of trajectories that ever reach p ≥ 0.9). The policy's search count is unmoored from it: 99.9% of sufficient trajectories keep searching, issuing a mean of 16.1 post-sufficiency searches; 66.3% of all search turns occur after sufficiency.

The 29.5% of trajectories that never reach p ≥ 0.9 have final readiness 0.53 on average when they fail (EM=0 mean pfinal = 0.53 vs 0.91 for EM=1) — under-retrieval exists, but it is the minority failure mode; the majority pattern is retrieving enough immediately and then spinning.

4 The repeated-query attractor

Criterion failures are position-dependent in a way that identifies the mechanism. The first search is nearly always sensible: novel (99.2%), progression-valid (100%), info-gaining (78.4%). By position ≥3, novel fails on 99.96% of turns and info_gain on 99.98% — the policy re-issues the same (or a near-duplicate) query and re-retrieves the same documents, turn after turn. The mean longest consecutive non-novel run is 16.0 turns under greedy decoding.

well_formed and relevant are position-independent: they measure query style (the policy pastes the question verbatim — 92.6% well_formed failure everywhere) and topicality (89.2% of queries are on-chain), not the loop. The loop is entirely captured by novel / info_gain / progression.

5 Readiness dynamics

Mean pt by turn position, split by final outcome: successful trajectories saturate readiness at the first search (p ≈ 0.9 at position 0) and stay flat — later searches add nothing the judge can see. Failed trajectories plateau near 0.5 and never recover, consistent with "retrieved the wrong entity's page and kept re-retrieving it" rather than progressive refinement. p0 = 0 for every trajectory (the judge treats no question as answerable without retrieval).

6 Which judge signals predict success?

Rank AUC of trajectory-level signals against EM (0.5 = chance). This is the Phase-1 discrimination readout that feeds criterion selection for the pen arm and validates pt for redist.

  • pfinal (AUC 0.74) and mean info_gain (AUC 0.69) carry real signal — supports using p-increments as the redist reward and info_gain in monitoring.
  • novel / progression / relevant (AUC 0.56) are weakly informative individually on this single-hop slice.
  • well_formed (AUC 0.49) has no discriminative power: penalizing it in pen would act as a near-uniform per-search tax on this policy — indistinguishable from the judge-free cost arm, at 400× the compute. Recommendation: drop well_formed from the pen penalty (keep it as a logged canary) unless the temp-1.0 collection shows otherwise.
  • Fewer searches predicts success only weakly (AUC 0.56) — search count alone is a blunt signal, which is the a-priori case for targeted (flag-gated) penalties over a plain cost.

7 Implications for the reward arms

  • pen: the actionable penalty surface is novel=0 (94% of turns under greedy, position-targeted, mechanism-aligned) and t > sufficiency_turn (66% of turns). Drop well_formed (§6). λ on the EM scale stays at 0.02–0.05: with ~10+ flagged turns per greedy trajectory the summed penalty must not swamp the EM outcome term at temp 1.0 (~5.4 turns, fewer flags).
  • redist: pt saturates at turn 1 on single-hop — increments will be front-loaded, which is exactly the intended credit shape (pay the turn that found the evidence, not the spin). Calibration check still pending on temp-1.0 multi-hop data.
  • cost: the attractor means even a blind per-search cost has a large target to cut; §6's weak search-count AUC is why pen may still beat it on accuracy retention.
  • Judge ops: 0/2000 JSON fallbacks in schema mode; ~45 min wall-clock for 2,000 trajectories on 4×H100 at concurrency 32 → comfortably inside the ~6M tok/step budget for training-time judging.
