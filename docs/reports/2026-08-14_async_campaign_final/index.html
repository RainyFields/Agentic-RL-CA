Throughput Is Not Learning — Async-Rollout 8B Campaign, Final Report

2026-08-14 · Qwen3-8B-Base · ASearcher Base-35k · 32-turn / 16k / 1k protocol · 4 campaign arms (2 engines × 2 estimators, 75 steps) + 5-estimator sweep · 7,152-q transfer suite, gpt-oss-120b judge

1 Key findings

  • The async engine's systems claims all hold — 2.01× end-to-end at matched config, 3–4× on the rollout phase, zombie generation 49.6% → 0, val parity at profile scale — but its ~2× faster per-step learning is a data-rate effect, not sample efficiency: at matched cumulative trajectories the old engine matches or beats it everywhere.
  • Old-engine arms win transfer across the board: wiki-answerable macro judge 0.550 (old turn-PPO) / 0.535 (old GRPO) vs 0.506 (async GRPO) / 0.445 (async turn-PPO). The async GRPO train-val "win" (0.529 vs 0.520) reverses on transfer.
  • Both async deficits are answer-coverage collapses, not accuracy losses. Conditional on answering, async turn-PPO is the most accurate arm in the campaign (judge 0.623) — but it never emits an answer on 30.2% of questions (old: 0.3%), ending trajectories by context exhaustion after ~17-turn searches. 720 of its 1,028 paired losses vs old turn-PPO are no-answer terminations.
  • Stale-drop censoring is ruled out (zero max_age drops in both engines, all arms, all 75 steps). Prime suspect for the late regression (async tPPO val 0.486 → 0.404 over steps 50–75): the critic × partial-rollout-resume interaction.
  • Estimator sweep (old engine, complete): turn-PPO wins (0.561) > GiGPO 0.535 > HCAPO 0.529 > GRPO 0.520 > token-PPO 0.426. GiGPO fully recovered from its step-25 val collapse (0.064 → 0.535); HCAPO delivers 0.529 at just 3.7 turns/question (best accuracy-per-turn); both late regressions in the campaign (token-PPO 0.447 → 0.426 and async turn-PPO) are critic-based arms.

Correction to earlier working notes. max_age stale-group drops were zero in both engines across all 75 steps of all four arms — stale censoring is not a mechanism of any observed difference. The operative data-side differences are: (1) release volume — async releases 1,200–1,700 traj/step vs 690–1,150 (cumulative 92–94k vs 55–69k over 75 steps) because the old engine's fixed batch geometry drops surplus completed groups; (2) completion mix — continuous slot backfill keeps long trajectories lockstep cycles would truncate or never collect; (3) fresher resumes — paused trajectories resume immediately rather than at the next 8-turn cycle; (4) fp16 engine numerics (token ids byte-exact by construction).

4 Per-step vs per-sample learning

By step, async arms reach val 0.32–0.43 by step 25 while the old arms sit at ~0.23. (Dips near step 26 in the train curves are pod-reclaim/OOM resume replays, not learning events.)

Re-indexing the same val points by cumulative released trajectories makes the async advantage disappear: old GRPO reaches 0.520 by ~38k trajectories; async GRPO needs ~92k to reach 0.529. Old turn-PPO reaches 0.561 at 69k; async turn-PPO peaks at 0.486 (61k) and falls to 0.404 at 94k. The per-step advantage is a data-rate effect — more trajectories per update at identical optimizer settings — with per-sample efficiency mildly worse.

  • Async turn-PPO's entire deficit is coverage. Paired per-question vs old turn-PPO (6,116 common questions): 1,028 lost, 318 gained — 720 of the 1,028 losses are no-answer terminations. No-answer trajectories average 17.2 turns with 0% at the 32-turn cap: they end via ctx_terminate — the policy searches until the 16k window fills without committing to an answer. No-answer concentrates on multihop (Musique 43%, 2Wiki 42%, Hotpot 31% vs TriviaQA/PopQA ~19%).
  • Async GRPO's paradox resolves the same way: answered-only accuracy matches old GRPO (0.573 vs 0.571); the transfer gap is 2× the no-answer rate (11.7% vs 6.2%) plus turn inflation (20–23-turn bucket: 35% vs 10%). It beats old GRPO on train-val because the 512-q val set matches the training distribution where long searches pay off.
  • The drift is training-time: async turn-PPO's own val fell 0.486 → 0.404 over steps 50–75 while train success kept rising. Prime suspect: critic × partial-rollout-resume — resumed trajectories carry returns realized under older policies; a turn-level critic bootstraps across the resume seam, and async's higher resume volume makes the value of "continue searching" systematically stale-optimistic. GRPO (no critic) shows the same direction at ~1/3 strength. Candidate fixes: behavior-IS staleness correction (parked item R2), answer-commitment shaping, resume-age cap for critic arms.

7 Comparability caveats

  • Async-arm evals ran on A100 (old-arm evals on H100); cross-arch greedy drift is within ±0.02 — the deficits are 3–8× that.
  • Greedy same-checkpoint reproducibility ±0.02; judge ≈ EM + 0.13, one-directional (judge✓EM✗ ≈ 930–975 items per arm, converse ≤ 3).
  • Async arms checkpointed every 5 steps (pod-reclaim replay bound), old arms every 25; replay differences are ≤5-step re-samples.

Final readings. GiGPO's step-25 collapse fully recovered (0.064 → 0.438 → 0.535, second place); its train success was normal (~0.45) throughout — the dip was a transient greedy-decode pathology (17.8 val turns at step 25), not estimator failure. token-PPO regressed 0.447 → 0.426 over steps 50–75 — the only old-engine arm to regress late; both late regressions in the campaign (this and async turn-PPO, §6) are critic-based arms. HCAPO is the turn-efficiency standout: 0.529 at 3.7 turns/question — half of turn-PPO's 7.5, ~4.5× leaner than GRPO/GiGPO — though its γ=0.95 is a deliberate protocol deviation (paper value; all other arms γ=1.0). val@25 was weakly predictive of val@75: the step-25 leader finished last, the step-25 collapse finished second.

9 Verdict & artifacts

Adopt the async collector for what it is — a 2× wall-clock engine with clean semantics (atomic groups, zero zombie generation, val parity at profile scale). Do not yet adopt it as the training default: its data distribution (more, longer, fresher-resumed trajectories) pushes policies toward non-committal search — mildly for GRPO, severely for critic-based turn-PPO. Fix order: (1) staleness/IS correction at the resume seam; (2) answer-commitment shaping or no-answer penalty; (3) re-run the turn-PPO arm. The sample-matched curves here are the baseline any fix must beat.
