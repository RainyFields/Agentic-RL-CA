\begin{abstract}
We evaluate the two finished 8B training arms --- token-level GRPO and turn-level PPO
(\texttt{gae\_turn}) --- at step 75 on the full ASearcher test suite: 7{,}152 questions
over 10 benchmarks, greedy decoding, the training environment's own wiki-18/e5 retrieval,
scored by strict exact match (the training reward), substring EM, and a gpt-oss-120b judge
with a frozen prompt. turn-PPO transfers ahead of GRPO on the wiki-answerable slice
(macro judge \hw{0.550 vs.\ 0.535}; strict EM 0.405 vs.\ 0.395), directionally consistent
with the training validation ordering (0.561 vs.\ 0.520) but with a much smaller margin ---
and it does so at \hw{2--4$\times$ fewer turns per question}. Strict EM understates true
accuracy by $\approx$0.13--0.14 with 99.7\% one-directional disagreement. The live-web trio
(GAIA / frames / xbench-deepsearch) floors for both arms, measuring the wiki-18 corpus gap
rather than the credit-assignment method.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Setup}
Checkpoints: \texttt{token\_grpo}\,/\,\texttt{turn\_ppo\_b0}
\texttt{\_qwen3-8b-base\_32turn\_fast75\_s0} at step 75 (unfiltered ASearcher Base-35k,
32-turn horizon, 16k prompt / 1024 response, top-5 wiki-18/e5 retrieval, fast config:
partial rollout 8/4 + dynamic token batching + chunked prefill). Evaluation:
\texttt{val\_only} greedy rollouts in the training environment; full trajectory dumps
retained. Scoring pipeline \texttt{scripts/asearcher/judge\_rollouts.py}: dump
$\rightarrow$ trajectory fold $\rightarrow$ gold join $\rightarrow$ concurrent judging
(gpt-oss-120b, TP4, mxfp4; frozen prompt \texttt{asearcher\_mbe\_v1}) $\rightarrow$
per-benchmark aggregation. The 7 \emph{wiki-answerable} benchmarks (NQ, TriviaQA, PopQA,
HotpotQA, 2WikiMultihopQA, Musique, Bamboogle; 6{,}115 questions) are reported separately
from the 3 \emph{live-web} benchmarks (GAIA, frames, xbench-deepsearch; 1{,}027 questions)
that require open-web retrieval our offline corpus cannot serve.

\section{Search efficiency}
GRPO runs essentially every question to near the 32-turn cap (16.5--18.1 average turns,
uniformly --- including single-hop questions answerable with one search), consistent with
its training-side turn re-inflation (14.6$\rightarrow$17.0 turns over steps 50--75) and
per-turn response collapse (54 tokens/turn). turn-PPO modulates its budget with difficulty:
the full distribution (Fig.~\ref{fig:turnsdist}) is \hw{bimodal} --- 44\% of its
trajectories answer within $\sim$2 turns (single-hop questions resolved immediately) with a
second mode at $\sim$13 turns for multi-hop --- while GRPO is unimodal at 16--19 turns for
\emph{everything} (medians 12 vs.\ 17, means 8.3 vs.\ 17.3). At equal-or-better accuracy
this is a \hw{2--4$\times$ cheaper inference-time policy}.

\section{Training dynamics}
The strategy split emerges during training, not at eval time (Fig.~\ref{fig:traindyn}).
Both arms' temperature-1 train success climbs 0.2$\rightarrow$0.5 nearly in lockstep ---
the training reward barely distinguishes them --- while their \emph{policies} diverge:
both inflate to $\sim$570 response tokens/turn by step 5, then GRPO collapses to
$\sim$54 tokens/turn (terse queries, many turns) while turn-PPO stabilizes at
$\sim$145 (fuller per-turn reasoning, fewer turns). The eval-time differences of the
preceding sections are the downstream signature of this divergence.

\section{Caveats}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item \hw{Single seed.} One run per arm; the wiki-answerable judge gap ($+0.015$) is
  small. Seed replication is queued before any headline claim.
  \item \hw{Unfiltered training data (parametric-recall confound).} Both arms trained
  without the closed-book filter. Measured on a 944-query sample of the training pool
  (early-training 8B, temperature 1, 5 attempts each; Fig.~\ref{fig:parametric}): the
  near-base policy solves 51.6\% of queries at least once, and \hw{54.8\% of those solved
  queries have at least one zero-search win} (27.3\% are solved \emph{only} by memory) ---
  43.9\% of all winning trajectories used no search at all. The training reward therefore
  partly pays for parametric recall rather than search skill. The trained checkpoints
  nearly always search at eval time (zero-search trajectories: GRPO 0.0\%, turn-PPO 1.6\%),
  but this does not prove retrieval \emph{dependence}, and the 4B \emph{filtered} campaign
  inverted the arm ordering. (For scale: the forced closed-book measurement on 4B found
  28.8\% of all queries parametric-answerable; the 8B in-env 28.3\% is a lower bound on the
  same quantity, so the true 8B rate is likely higher. The forced 8B closed-book pass is
  queued.)
  \item \hw{Live-web $\neq$ method comparison.} GAIA/frames/xbench need the open web; our
  retriever serves a 2018 wiki snapshot.
  \item An earlier (2026-08-04 morning) claim that transfer \emph{reverses} the training-val
  ordering was based on a mis-computed turn-PPO macro (0.355) and is superseded by the
  full-dump judged numbers here.
  \item \hw{Pending.} The three in-flight arms (\texttt{hcapo\_ans}, \texttt{token\_ppo},
  \texttt{gigpo}) receive the identical eval + judge pass at step 75, extending this to the
  full five-arm estimator comparison.
\end{itemize}
